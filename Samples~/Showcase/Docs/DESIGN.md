# BRIO: FIRST HARMONIC – design and art direction

The one-level showcase game for SFX FORGE. This document is the single source of truth for what the
game is, how it looks and how it sounds. Numbers marked *(target)* are pacing references; the gameplay
programmer owns the final values. Everything else is decided: do not add options.

Hard rules that apply to every section below:

- **Original.** Every name, shape, colour, layout and sound here is invented for this project. Nothing
  may be renamed, reshaped or retuned to resemble an existing game.
- **Procedural.** No imported textures, models, fonts, audio files or third-party assets. Meshes,
  sprites and materials come from the `ArtKit`; scenes and assets are generated by the editor builder.
- **All sound is SFX FORGE at runtime.** `SfxPlayer`, `SfxMusicPlayer` or clips baked from `SfxPreset`.
  The game must run with no device and no MIDI connected.

---

## 1. The pitch

**BRIO: FIRST HARMONIC** is a 2D momentum platformer set in Oscilla, a land whose ground is made of
sound waves. You are Brio, a small round spark who gathers speed over sine hills, tucks into a ball to
ride loops, and winds up into a burst when the ground runs out of push. The faster you go, the fuller
the band plays – the level is a song you perform by running it well.

| Thing | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Character | **Brio** | A round, sun-yellow spark-creature. "Con brio" = with vigour. |
| Collectible | **Chime** | A small cyan diamond that rings a note when collected. |
| World | **Oscilla** | A dusk landscape whose hills are waveforms (sine, saw, square). |
| Level | **First Harmonic** | The one level. About 1 000 m, 60-90 s on a good run. |
| Spring | **Woofer** | A speaker cone set in the ground that punches Brio away. |
| Hazard (static) | **Static** | A jagged burr of noise: spikes shaped like a noise waveform. |
| Enemy | **Clipper** | A flat-topped, square-wave walker that patrols a short stretch. |
| Checkpoint | **Tuning Fork** | A two-pronged post that lights up and hums when passed. |
| Goal | **The Resonator** | A tall arch of two giant fork prongs; running through it ends the level. |
| Roll | **Tuck** | Brio curls into a ball. |
| Charged dash | **Wind-Up** / **Burst** | Charge on the spot, release to launch. |

### How Brio is drawn (all from ArtKit primitives)

- **Body:** a circle, 1.0 m across, Brio Yellow, with the standard ink outline (section 4).
- **Crest:** one teardrop/leaf shape on top of the head, leaning backwards, Ember Orange. It leans
  further back the faster Brio moves (0° at rest to 50° at top speed) – the speed read on the character.
- **Eyes:** two tall Ink ellipses set towards the facing side; they squash to flat lines when hurt.
- **Feet:** two small Ember Orange ovals that cycle around an ellipse under the body while running;
  the cycle rate follows ground speed. At top speed they blur into a single flattened oval ring.
- **Tucked:** crest, eyes and feet hide; the body becomes the circle with one Ember Orange arc stripe
  across it so the rotation is visible. The ball spins at the rate its ground speed implies.
- **Trail:** above 70 % of top speed, a tapered Brio Yellow ribbon (a generated strip mesh, not a
  texture) follows Brio, fading to transparent over about 2 m.
- Brio's collision size is a 0.5 m radius standing, 0.4 m tucked.

## 2. Abilities and how they feel

Controls (legacy Input Manager, which is what the project has enabled; keyboard and gamepad):
left/right = move, down = tuck / crouch, one **Jump** button (Space / gamepad south). Nothing else.

| Ability | How it should feel |
|---|---|
| **Run** | Brio starts gently and takes about two seconds of flat ground to reach cruising speed (~10 m/s *target*). Acceleration is modest; deceleration when you let go is slow (a long, readable coast); pressing against your motion brakes hard with a short skid. Never twitchy. |
| **Build speed** | Flat running tops out at cruise. Anything above cruise is *earned* from slopes, Tucks, Woofers and Bursts, up to a hard cap (~22 m/s *target*). "Speed" everywhere outside the movement code – the meter, the music's intensity, the wind, the trail, the results' top speed – is the speed of travel: along the ground, or sideways in the air, never above the cap. Rising and falling do not count, so a Woofer's throw or a fall into a pit is not "fast". Speed above cruise bleeds off slowly on flat ground, so a good line keeps it. |
| **Slopes** | Gravity is felt along the ground: downhill pulls you faster, uphill drags. A hill you walk up slowly is a hill you fly over when you arrive fast. Running too slowly on a steep wall or ceiling makes Brio lose grip and drop – speed is what holds you to the ground. |
| **Loops** | A loop is just a very steep slope: enter fast enough and Brio sticks all the way round; enter too slowly and Brio falls off the wall. The camera does not rotate. Every loop has a visible run-up that gives enough speed if the player simply holds forward. |
| **Tuck (roll)** | Press down while moving: Brio curls up. Tucked, slopes act *more* strongly (downhill gives more, uphill takes more) and friction is low, but you cannot accelerate with the stick. Tucking down a hill is the fastest thing in the game. Tucked Brio defeats Clippers on contact. Untucks automatically when nearly stopped. |
| **Wind-Up and Burst** | Standing still, hold down and tap Jump: Brio winds up on the spot, spinning. Each further tap adds charge (up to about six taps); charge slowly leaks. Release down: Brio launches tucked along the ground, from brisk (one tap) to nearly the speed cap (full). It should feel like stretching and letting go of a rubber band. |
| **Jump** | Leaves the ground along the surface normal, so jumping off a slope throws you with the slope. Holding the button gives full height (~3.5 m *target*); releasing early cuts the rise short, immediately. Brio is tucked in the air after a jump and defeats Clippers. Air control is moderate: you can steer, not reverse instantly. |
| **Woofer bounce** | Instant, strong, fixed impulse along the Woofer's axis; overrides whatever you were doing. Vertical Woofers throw ~7 m high; diagonal ones give height and speed; horizontal ones set you to well above cruise. The cone visibly punches out and wobbles back. |
| **Getting hit** | Touching Static, or a Clipper while not tucked, knocks Brio back in a short arc. All carried Chimes (at most 24 are spawned) scatter outward in a fan, bounce, blink and vanish after ~4 s; they can be re-collected. Brio blinks and is invulnerable for ~2 s. Hit with **zero** Chimes, or falling into a pit: Brio stops where it is, blinks out for 0.8 s and reappears at the last Tuning Fork with zero Chimes (a blink, not a fade: the art has no fades on the character). There are no lives; the timer keeps running. |
| **Defeating a Clipper** | Hit it while tucked (rolling or jumping): it pops into four square shards, and if Brio was airborne it gets a small rebound upward. |
| **Goal** | Running through the Resonator takes control away; Brio brakes to a stop over ~15 m while the results panel (Chimes, time, top speed) appears in the upper half of the view, above the braking Brio. Jump restarts the level. |

Camera: orthographic side view, 9 m half-height. Follows Brio with a look-ahead in the direction of
travel that grows with speed (up to 6 m), so there is always more screen ahead than behind. Vertical
follow is damped; it never snaps.

## 3. The level: First Harmonic

Left to right, about 1 000 m. Ground height wanders over ~40 m in total. Each section is a named piece
of data in the level definition (name, start x, end x) so the music and HUD can ask "which section is
the player in?". What the music does in each – how full the band is at the least, and whether a pattern
is held – is the `MusicDirector`'s own table (section 5, "Music").

| # | Section | x range (m) | Length | Purpose | The player should feel | Music |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Soft Start** | 0 – 90 | 90 | Teach running and jumping. Flat ground, two low steps, three lines of Chimes at running height, one arc of Chimes that traces a jump. No hazards. | Safe; "oh, it keeps accelerating". | The song in its own order |
| 2 | **Rolling Sines** | 90 – 220 | 130 | First slopes. Three sine hills, each taller than the last; Chimes hang in the valleys along the line a Tucked Brio takes. One Clipper on the flat top of hill two, easy to jump. | Speed comes from the ground; tucking downhill is a gift. | The song in its own order |
| 3 | **Full Cycle** | 220 – 340 | 120 | First loop. A long downhill run-up, one loop (radius 6 m), Chimes placed around the inside of the loop, then a gentle rise that bleeds the speed off. **Tuning Fork 1 at x = 335.** | The first "wow"; the world turned over and I stayed on. | The song in its own order |
| 4 | **Woofer Steps** | 340 – 460 | 120 | Springs. A staircase of square-wave terraces too tall to jump; a vertical Woofer at the foot of each (three), Chime columns showing each flight. Ends with one diagonal Woofer that throws Brio across a gap onto the next section. | Bouncy, rhythmic, playful; a breather from speed. | The song in its own order |
| 5 | **Static Field** | 460 – 600 | 140 | The hazard stretch. **Tuning Fork 2 at x = 466, on the landing flat before the hazards.** Saw-tooth ridges with a Static burr below each one's drop, four Clippers on the flats, two short pits (respawn pits). Slower, precise; Chimes mark the safe line. | Tension; I have something to lose now. | Pattern E, the breakdown, held |
| 6 | **Split Signal** | 600 – 800 | 200 | High route and low route. **Tuning Fork 3 at x = 604, before the hill** (a respawn gets another try at the high route). A ramp at the start: arriving fast (or using the diagonal Woofer at its lip) lands Brio on the **high route** – floating sine platforms, long Chime arcs, no enemies, fast. Missing it drops to the **low route** – a trough with three Clippers, two Static burrs and a Woofer at x ≈ 700 that offers a second chance to reach the high route. Both rejoin at x = 790. | High: flying, rewarded. Low: busy but fair, never a punishment box. | The song in its own order again |
| 7 | **Overdrive** | 800 – 960 | 160 | Speed finale. A horizontal Woofer starts it, then a long downhill, two loops back to back (radius 6 m, then 7 m), a final valley with a second horizontal Woofer (the loops cost too much speed for the launch without it) and a launch ramp that throws Brio in a long arc over a field of Chimes. No hazards at all. | Everything learned, at full speed; the whole band. | The song in its own order; then F, the turnaround, is cued when Brio leaves the launch ramp (x ≈ 930) and held |
| 8 | **The Resonator** | 960 – 1 000 | 40 | The goal. Flat landing strip, the Resonator arch at x = 980, 20 m of run-out. | Arrival; a clean ending chord. | F stays held until the goal; there the song stops; fanfare (section 5) |

Level rules:

- **Chime count:** 150 in the level (± 5). The HUD shows collected / carried, not a total.
- **Pits** exist only in Static Field (two) and under the high route (which is the low route, not a
  pit). Anything below y = lowest ground − 15 m is a respawn.
- **Never blind:** no hazard is placed where the player arrives faster than cruise speed without at
  least 12 m of clear sight line. Overdrive and Full Cycle are hazard-free on purpose.
- **Slow players finish too:** every loop and ramp can be retried by walking back; nothing soft-locks.
  A Wind-Up Burst from standing at the foot of any loop is enough to clear it.
- **Chimes are the tutorial.** There is no text in the level. Lines of Chimes show the intended path.
- The level is described as data (a list of terrain pieces and placed objects) in one readable file,
  and the editor builder turns it into the scene. No hand-edited scene.

## 4. Art direction: "Signal at Dusk"

A flat, unlit, vector look: solid shapes of flat colour on a dusk gradient sky, as if the level were
drawn on an oscilloscope that learned about colour. The world is made of waveforms – that is the one
idea, and it is held everywhere.

### Palette (the only colours in the game)

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#161226` | Outlines of interactive things; eyes; HUD text shadow. Darkest value in the game. |
| Night Plum | `#2A1E4A` | Sky, top of the gradient. Also the HUD panel fill (at 70 % alpha). |
| Dusk Magenta | `#7A2E6B` | Sky, middle of the gradient. |
| Horizon Peach | `#F2A27C` | Sky, bottom of the gradient; the sun disc is a lighter tint of it. |
| Far Violet | `#4A3478` | Farthest background wave layer. |
| Mid Violet | `#3A2A66` | Middle background wave layer. |
| Near Indigo | `#2E2256` | Nearest background wave layer. |
| Ground Teal | `#1F5C63` | Terrain body. |
| Ground Deep | `#17454F` | Terrain strata bands (the alternate stripe inside the body). |
| Signal Cream | `#F6EBD0` | The terrain's top line; HUD text; Clipper eyes. The brightest neutral. |
| Brio Yellow | `#FFD23F` | Brio's body and trail. Used for nothing else in the world. |
| Ember Orange | `#FF8A3C` | Brio's crest, feet and tuck stripe. |
| Chime Cyan | `#36C5FF` | Chimes and their sparkle. Used for nothing else in the world. |
| Woofer Violet | `#C48BFF` | Woofers (cone and rim). |
| Fork Green | `#7BE07B` | Tuning Forks once lit; the Resonator; the "safe" end of the speed meter. |
| Static Red | `#FF5A36` | Static burrs and Clippers. Red means "this hurts", always and only. |
| Dormant Grey | `#8C86A8` | Unlit Tuning Forks; defeated-Clipper shards; disabled UI. |

The five saturated accents (Static Red, Brio Yellow, Chime Cyan, Fork Green, Woofer Violet) are
SFX FORGE's own five engine colours. That is deliberate: the game wears the instrument's colours.
All colours live in one `Palette` class in the ArtKit; no colour literal appears anywhere else.

### Shape language

- **Friendly things are round:** Brio (circle), Woofers (ellipse cone on a rounded base), Chimes
  (a diamond – a square turned 45° with slightly rounded corners, 0.5 m tall, spinning slowly on its
  vertical axis by scaling its width).
- **Dangerous things are angular:** Static is a jagged noise waveform (8-12 irregular spikes, 1 m
  tall, on a 2 m base); a Clipper is a square wave – a 1.2 m flat-topped block on two stubby legs with
  two Signal Cream square eyes.
- **Structures are forks:** a Tuning Fork is a 3 m stem with a U of two prongs; the Resonator is the
  same shape at 12 m, wide enough to run through.
- **Terrain is waveforms:** hills are sine curves, ridges are saw-tooth, terraces are square waves,
  loops are circles. No other terrain profiles.
- No gradients inside objects, no textures, no patterns of squares. The only gradient is the sky.

### Line and outline rules

- **Anything you can touch (other than the ground) has an Ink outline; nothing else does.** Brio,
  Chimes, Woofers, Static, Clippers, Tuning Forks, the Resonator: outline 0.06 m, made by the ArtKit
  as a slightly larger Ink copy of the mesh behind it. Background and terrain have no outline.
- **The ground is read by its top line:** every walkable surface carries a Signal Cream strip, 0.2 m
  thick, following the surface exactly (including round the inside of loops). Below it the body is
  Ground Teal with horizontal Ground Deep strata bands, each 0.6 m thick, every 1.8 m, in world space
  (the bands stay level; they do not follow the slope). The player always knows where the floor is
  from the cream line alone.
- Loops draw their far half behind Brio and their near half in front, in the same colours.

### Background and parallax (back to front)

| Layer | Content | Parallax factor (1 = moves with the world) |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | Vertical gradient quad: Night Plum → Dusk Magenta → Horizon Peach. A sun disc low on the right. | 0 (fixed to the camera) |
| Far | A slow, wide sine wave silhouette, Far Violet. | 0.1 |
| Mid | A square-wave mesa silhouette, Mid Violet. | 0.25 |
| Near | A saw-tooth ridge silhouette, Near Indigo. | 0.45 |
| Play | Terrain, objects, Brio. | 1 |

There is no foreground layer: nothing ever passes in front of the player. Background layers are three
flat silhouettes with no detail, all darker and less saturated than anything in the play layer.

### Readability at speed

- **Value separation:** the background lives in the dark third of the value range; the terrain body
  in the dark-middle; the cream top line and every interactive accent in the bright third.
- **One colour, one meaning.** Yellow is you, cyan is "collect", violet is "bounce", red is "hurts",
  green is "progress". No decorative use of these five colours anywhere.
- **Size floor:** nothing interactive is smaller than 0.5 m. Hazards are at least 1 m.
- **Look-ahead camera** (section 2) plus the "never blind" level rule (section 3).
- **No screen shake, no flashes of the full screen.** Feedback is local: a squash, a pop, a ring.
- **Motion is the decoration:** Brio's crest lean, foot blur and trail; a Woofer's wobble; a Chime's
  spin; a collected Chime's small expanding cyan circle (0.2 s). Effects are generated meshes, 12
  particles at most per burst, palette colours only.

### Lighting and materials

- No lights, no shadows, no post-processing. The scene is fully unlit.
- **One material** for all opaque geometry: an unlit vertex-colour material created by the ArtKit.
  One more of the same kind with alpha blending for the trail, sparkles, fades and the HUD panels.
  Colour is always written into mesh vertex colours from `Palette`; materials are never tinted per
  object.
- Depth is by sorting order / z only: sky, far, mid, near, loop-back, objects, Brio, loop-front,
  effects, HUD.
- Squash and stretch is the animation system: transforms only, no skeletal or sprite-sheet animation.

### UI

- **HUD** (screen-space, always visible in play):
  - top-left: a Chime icon and the carried Chime count, three digits;
  - top-centre: the time, `m:ss.cc`;
  - bottom-left: the **speed meter** – a row of 12 upright bars like a level meter. Bars fill left to
    right with speed (12 = the speed cap): bars 1-7 Fork Green, 8-10 Brio Yellow, 11-12 Static Red.
    Unfilled bars are Dormant Grey at 40 % alpha;
  - top-right: the current section's name, shown for 3 s on entering a section, then faded out.
- Text is Signal Cream with a 1-pixel Ink drop shadow, uppercase only. The font is a stroke font
  generated by the art kit (`StrokeFont`), so nothing in the showcase is a non-generated asset.
- Panels are Night Plum at 70 % alpha with square corners and a 2-pixel Signal Cream line along the
  bottom edge only.
- **Screens:** a title card over the live level ("BRIO: FIRST HARMONIC", "PRESS JUMP") and the results
  panel at the goal (Chimes, time, top speed, "PRESS JUMP TO PLAY AGAIN"). No menus, no options.

## 5. Audio direction

Rule one: the sound code must be the easiest code in the project to read. One `GameEvents` seam (C#
events raised by gameplay, never by audio), one audio class that subscribes to them, and one file that
holds every sound-effect patch as a named patch (`Make(...)`, a wrapper that gives every value of `SfxPatch.Create(...)` a name) so a reader can see the whole sound
set on one screen. Gameplay code never references SFX FORGE types.

The tune is the demo song (E minor, patterns A-F): `Assets/SfxDemo/DemoSong.asset`, of which the showcase carries its own copy, `Song/DemoSong.asset`. Every pitched effect is tuned to
E minor pentatonic (E G A B D) so effects sound like part of the band.

### Sound-effect events

> **As built.** The table below is the design brief for each sound. The events that exist, with their
> payloads, are in `ARCHITECTURE.md` section 3.1 and `Scripts/Core/GameEvents.cs`; the differences from
> this table: rows 5 and 15 have no event (`RollLoop`, `SpeedChanged` and `TuckEnded` do not exist –
> the audio reads `IPlayerState` every frame instead); `Respawned` and `SectionEntered` carry a
> `SectionId`; `GameStarted` and `FinalLaunch` were added for the music; and row 9, the scatter, is
> played Live (each ping has its own pitch), not Baked.

"Live" = an `SfxPlayer` playing the patch through the native engine (needed where pitch, cutoff or
level is modulated while it sounds). "Baked" = an `AudioClip` from `SfxPreset.Bake`, played by an
`AudioSource` (right for short one-shots that fire often).

| # | Event (`GameEvents`) | When | Character of the sound | Start from | How |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Jumped` | Brio leaves the ground by jumping | A quick, bright upward chirp; light, never shrill; under 0.2 s. | POWER-UP (shorter) | Baked |
| 2 | `Landed(impactSpeed)` | Touching ground after being airborne | A soft, low thump; velocity follows impact speed; silent below a small threshold. | THUD | Baked, volume by impact |
| 3 | `Footstep(speed01)` | Each foot-cycle step while running (not tucked) | A tiny dry tick; rate comes from the foot cycle (one stride length, `DesignTargets.StrideLength`, drives both the event and the legs), so it accelerates with Brio; pitch rises a little with speed; alternates two slightly different pitches (left/right). | FOOTSTEP (shorter, quieter) | Baked, two clips |
| 4 | `TuckStarted` | Entering a roll on the ground | A short zip downward, like a zipper closing. | LASER (softer, shorter) | Baked |
| 5 | `RollLoop(speed01)` / `TuckEnded` | Continuous while rolling on the ground | A low held hum whose pitch and brightness follow speed; fades out on untuck or leaving the ground. | ENGINE HUM | Live: `pitchSemitones`, `cutoffOctaves`, `level` |
| 6 | `WindUpCharged(charge01)` | Each charge tap of the Wind-Up | A rev that steps up in pitch with every tap (0 → +12 semitones across full charge) and gets brighter; held while charging. | ENGINE HUM / WARP | Live: retrigger with `semitones`, `fm` rising |
| 7 | `BurstReleased(charge01)` | Releasing the Wind-Up | A punchy noise whoosh with a falling tail; louder and longer with charge. | WHOOSH + PUNCH character | Baked, volume by charge |
| 8 | `ChimeCollected(combo)` | Picking up a Chime | A clear, glassy two-tone ping. Each Chime within 1.5 s of the last raises the pitch one step up E minor pentatonic (E G A B D E …), capped two octaves up; the combo resets after 1.5 s of none. This is the signature sound – it must be lovely fifty times in a row. | COIN | Live `Play(patch, velocity, semitones)` |
| 9 | `ChimesScattered(count)` | Brio is hit while carrying Chimes | A quick downward spill of five to eight COIN-like pings, falling in pitch, slightly crushed – the collect sound running backwards and broken. | COIN (CRUSH up) | Baked, a short cascade of retriggers |
| 10 | `WooferBounced` | Any Woofer fires | A fat, rubbery "boing": a low tone with a fast upward pitch sweep and a little wobble. The most characterful one-shot in the game. | WARP / POWER-UP at low FREQ with LFO_PITCH | Baked |
| 11 | `Hurt` | Brio takes a hit (with or without Chimes) | A harsh, short, falling buzz; unmistakably "wrong"; the only ugly sound in the game. | ERROR / ZAP | Baked |
| 12 | `ClipperDefeated` | A Clipper is popped | A crunchy pop with a metallic ring; satisfying, short. | METAL HIT + PUNCH | Baked |
| 13 | `CheckpointReached(index)` | Passing an unlit Tuning Fork | A struck fork: a pure, long, ringing tone with reverb, on E; then the music's bell answer (below). | METAL HIT (pure, long DECAY, REVERB) | Baked |
| 14 | `GoalReached` | Running through the Resonator | A big rising shimmer into the fanfare (below). | POWER-UP (long, REVERB) | Baked |
| 15 | `SpeedChanged(speed01)` – wind | Continuous, from 60 % of top speed | Filtered noise wind: silent below 60 %, then level and cutoff rise with speed; smoothed so it never clicks. | WIND | Live: `level`, `cutoffOctaves` |
| 16 | `Skidded` | Braking hard against motion on the ground | A short, gritty scrape. | CRACKLE (short) | Baked |
| 17 | `Respawned` | Reappearing at a Tuning Fork | A gentle upward warp, soft and reassuring. | WARP (quiet) | Baked |
| 18 | `UiConfirmed` | Pressing Jump on the title card or results panel | A clean, single short blip. | SELECT | Baked |
| 19 | `SectionEntered(index)` | Crossing a section boundary | No sound effect – it drives the music only. | – | – |

Mixing rules: effects sit above the music; no effect is longer than 1.5 s except the two loops (5, 15)
and the fork (13). At most one instance of each looping sound. Footsteps are the quietest sound in
the game. Chime pings never stack more than four voices.

> **As built.** The volumes are the last column of the table at the bottom of `SoundLibrary.cs`, and
> the music's is `MusicDirector.musicVolume` (0.36). They were set from measurements, not by ear: the
> whole mix, at full listener volume, has to stay under 0.95 of full scale in every recorded take
> (`AUDIO-NOTES.md` section 4, "The mix"). Nobody has judged the balance by listening yet.

### Music

One `SfxMusicPlayer` playing `DemoSong`, driven by one small `MusicDirector` class.

- **The song plays as it was arranged.** The demo song has a 16-bar arrangement,
  A B A B C D C D E E A B C D C F, and the level lets it walk through that, round and round. The
  game does not choose the song's bars; it chooses how much of the band plays them. (The first build
  cued and held one pattern per section, A B C D E C D, with floors of 0.15 to 0.70: at running speed
  that was the rhythm section on a one-bar loop, not the demo song.)
- **Intensity follows speed, from a floor.** Every instrument of the song has a layer mark and plays
  only when the intensity is at or above it. Each section has an *intensity floor*: the intensity when
  Brio stands still. Target = the floor at rest, 1 at the speed cap, a straight line between
  (`Mathf.Lerp(floor, 1, Speed01)`). The value sent to `SfxMusicPlayer.intensity` is smoothed: it
  rises quickly (0.6 s across the whole range) and falls slowly (2.5 s), so layers arrive with the
  rush and linger after it.

  The song's layer marks (the mark on the device, 0..127, divided by 127):

  | Kick | Hi-hat | FM bass | Snare | Strings and choir | FM keys | Brass | Bells |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 0.16 | 0.28 | 0.39 | 0.47 | 0.57 | 0.71 | 0.79 |

  So every floor below keeps the rhythm section (kick, hat, bass, snare) playing when Brio stands
  still; from Full Cycle to Overdrive the strings and choir are there too, in Overdrive the keys as well; and
  moving brings in the keys, then the brass, then the bells.
- **Two places hold a pattern** with `CuePattern` (which changes on the next bar line, so it is always
  musical), cued again on every bar line for as long as the hold lasts: Static Field holds E, the
  breakdown, while Brio is in it, and the last launch holds F, the turnaround, until the goal. When
  a hold ends the arrangement walks on from the bar it is in.

| Section | Intensity floor | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Title card | 0.42, fixed until the game starts | the song's own order |
| Soft Start | 0.42 | the song's own order |
| Rolling Sines | 0.45 | the song's own order |
| Full Cycle | 0.50 | the song's own order |
| Woofer Steps | 0.50 | the song's own order |
| Static Field | 0.50 | E (4) held |
| Split Signal | 0.55 | the song's own order: the hold ends |
| Overdrive | 0.60 | the song's own order, then F (5) held from the launch ramp |
| The Resonator | 0.42 | whatever is held stays held (F), until the goal stops the song |

- **On hurt:** the band flinches. Intensity is forced to 0 at once (only the kick remains) and the
  music volume dips to 50 %; both recover over 2 s through the normal smoothing. No pattern change.
- **On respawn:** the music does what it does on entering the section the Tuning Fork stands in (a
  respawn in Static Field holds E again; anywhere else a hold ends), and the intensity starts from
  that section's floor.
- **At a checkpoint:** the music answers the fork with three BELLS notes (music-kit sound 13) played
  through `PlaySound` – E, B, E an octave up, a sixteenth apart – on top of the running song, and the
  intensity is lifted to at least 0.8 for one bar, which is above every layer mark: the whole band,
  bells included. The song itself does not stop or change.
- **At the goal:** the song stops (`Stop`), then a fanfare is played live on the music kit with
  `PlaySound`: BRASS (sound 5) plays E – G – B – E (up), an eighth note apart at the song's tempo,
  with BELLS (13) doubling the last note, STRINGS (15) holding an E chord root under it, and one
  TIMPANI (0) hit on the last note. After the fanfare there is silence except UI sounds. Restarting
  starts the song again at pattern A.
- The showcase does not edit the demo's `DemoSong.asset`. It has its own copy in `Song/` (as built:
  `Song/DemoSong.bytes` and the asset the builder writes from it) and leaves the original untouched.

## 6. What this showcase must prove about SFX FORGE

The technical director checks these at the end.

1. **Zero audio files.** There is no `.wav`, `.ogg`, `.mp3` or other audio asset anywhere under
   `Assets/Showcase`; every effect and all music is produced by SFX FORGE at runtime, and the game
   plays correctly with no device and no MIDI connected.
2. **A whole game's sound set fits on one screen.** Every sound effect is a named
   patch (a named-argument wrapper round `SfxPatch.Create(...)`) in a single, commented file – readable, tweakable, and copyable into another
   project without touching anything else.
3. **Live modulation is what a synth gives you that samples cannot.** At least four sounds change
   while they sound or per trigger from gameplay values: the Chime combo pitch, the Wind-Up rev, the
   roll hum and the wind.
4. **Both playback routes are shown, and when to use each.** Baked `SfxPreset` clips for frequent
   one-shots; live `SfxPlayer` for modulated sounds – with a comment at each choice saying why.
5. **Adaptive music in a handful of lines.** The song plays in its own order; a floor per section and
   speed drive `intensity`; the breakdown and the turnaround are held with `CuePattern`;
   hurt, checkpoint and goal each do something audible with `PlaySound`, volume or `Stop` – all in one
   small `MusicDirector` a reader can take in at once.
6. **Clean seam, easy to lift out.** Gameplay raises `GameEvents`; only the audio folder references
   `CatSplat.SfxForge`. Outside the audio folder only `Editor/ShowcaseAudio.cs` and `Tests/ShowcaseAudioTests.cs`
   name the audio code; deleting the folder, those two files and the builder's two calls leaves a silent
   game. Copying the folder into another project needs ten small files of `Scripts/Core` or new event hookups
   (`ARCHITECTURE.md` 7.7).
7. **Reproducible from code.** The scene, presets and art are generated by the editor builder in
   batchmode, a `SelfTest` passes headless, and the existing SfxDemo checks still pass.

## Appendix: structure the team builds against

The folders, assemblies and namespaces that were built are in `ARCHITECTURE.md` section 2
(`SfxShowcase.Core`, `.Gameplay`, `.Audio`, `.Level`, `.Art`; the HUD lives in `Scripts/Art/Hud`).
These conventions were set at the start and still hold.

Conventions:

- Units are metres and seconds; 1 Unity unit = 1 m; the level runs along +x, up is +y, z is only
  for draw order.
- `GameEvents` is a static class of C# events; the names and payloads as built are in `ARCHITECTURE.md` 3.1.
  Values named `…01` are normalised 0..1 (speed ÷ speed cap, or charge ÷ full charge).
- Tunable numbers live in serialised fields or one settings object per system with a tooltip each;
  no magic numbers in logic.
- Nothing in `Assets/Showcase` depends on `Assets/SfxDemo`, code or assets (as built: the song is a copy
  inside the showcase's folder, so the folder works in any project and at any path).
